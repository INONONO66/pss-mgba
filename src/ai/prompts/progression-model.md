Progression Model
- Game progress means triggering events at specific locations, not merely crossing distance.
- Badges and resolved story events unlock travel options, NPC permissions, services, or new map access.
- New area access usually appears after a blocking story event clears, an NPC changes state, or an item/key permission exists.
- Walking onto specific tiles, talking to specific NPCs, having specific items, or entering a map can trigger events.
- A location that seemed blocked can become valid later, but only after observed state shows a relevant change.
- Treat progress as state transitions: changed dialog, moved blockers, new inventory, healed party, battle start, or map transition.

=== PARTY BUILDING PRINCIPLES ===

Team Composition:
- Build a balanced team of 4-6 Pokemon covering as many types as possible.
- Prioritize type coverage: Water, Electric, Ground, Psychic, Ice, and Fire/Flying cover nearly all threats.
- Every gym has a type specialty. Before challenging a gym, make sure you have at least one Pokemon with super-effective moves against that type.
- Psychic type is the strongest offensive type in Gen 1; if you can catch an Abra or find a Psychic type, it is worth training.
- Nidoran M/F evolves with Moon Stone into Nidoking/Nidoqueen, both learn many TMs and are versatile team anchors.

HM Coverage:
- You need Pokemon that can learn Cut, Fly, Surf, and Strength to finish the game.
- Fly requires a Flying type (Pidgey line, Spearow line, or certain others). Catch one early.
- Surf requires a Water type. If your starter is not Water, catch one before you need to cross water routes.
- Cut and Strength can be taught to many Pokemon; assign them to a less critical team member.
- Flash is optional but makes dark caves much easier.

When to Catch vs. When to Run:
- Catch a Pokemon if it fills a type gap, gives HM access, or counters an upcoming gym.
- Do not catch duplicates of species you already own.
- Run from wild battles that serve no purpose (not catching, not grinding, no new species).
- If Poke Balls are low (under 5), only catch high-priority targets.

Leveling and Grinding:
- Keep your main team within 2-3 levels of the area's wild Pokemon and trainers.
- If your lead is 3+ levels below area trainers or the next gym leader, grind on wild Pokemon near a Pokemon Center.
- Fight trainer battles whenever possible; they give more EXP and money than wild battles.
- Spread EXP across your main team, not one overleveled Pokemon.

=== RESOURCE MANAGEMENT ===

Money:
- Trainer battles are the primary income source. Fight every trainer you can reach.
- Keep a reserve ($500 early, $2000 mid-game, $5000 late game) for emergencies.
- Sell unneeded items (nuggets, extra TMs you will not use) for cash.

Inventory Checklist (maintain these minimums):
- Poke Balls: 10+ when exploring new areas with uncaught Pokemon.
- Healing items (Potion/Super Potion/Hyper Potion): at least 5.
- Status cures (Antidote, Parlyz Heal, or Full Heal): 2-3 before caves or poison-heavy areas.
- Escape Rope: 1-2 before entering any cave or long dungeon.
- Repels: optional but valuable in long caves. Super Repel has the best cost-per-step ratio.
- Revives: stock 3-5 before gym leaders and important battles.

When items run low, visit the nearest Poke Mart before continuing. New cities often have better mart inventories.

=== GYM PREPARATION ===

Before entering any gym:
- Heal your full party at the Pokemon Center.
- Ensure you have healing items and status cures.
- Check your team for type advantages against the gym's specialty.
- If no Pokemon has a super-effective move, consider catching or training one that does.
- Gym leaders have higher-level Pokemon than the area; your team should be within 2-3 levels of the leader's ace.

=== SHOP AWARENESS ===

Poke Marts stock different items depending on the city. In general:
- Early cities: Poke Ball, Potion, Antidote, Parlyz Heal.
- Mid cities: Great Ball, Super Potion, Escape Rope, Repel.
- Late cities: Ultra Ball, Hyper Potion, Full Heal, Revive, Max Repel.
- The largest department store is in Celadon City and sells evolution stones, TMs, and rare items.
When arriving in a new city, check the Poke Mart to see what is available and restock.
